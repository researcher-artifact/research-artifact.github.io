    Inter-rater reliability: Fleiss' kappa

    Agreement among the three raters (Researcher 1, OpenAI GPT and Anthropic Claude) for each dimension and coding stage of the thematic analysis. The values are recomputed in the browser from the coding sheets, following the method described in the article. Click a value of κ to walk through its calculation below.

    Full agreement counts the studies in which the three raters gave the same complete combination of labels: raw compares the original text character for character, normalised compares the combinations after normalisation and equivalences, and recovered is the difference. Pairwise agreement is the share of studies in which each pair of raters agrees; the mean of the three pairwise rates equals the observed agreement.

    How it is calculated

    Fleiss' κ measures agreement among m raters who independently assign each of N subjects to nominal categories. Here m = 3 and the subjects are the primary studies. Each dimension allows several labels per study, so each rater's complete combination of labels is treated as one category: agreement means the same complete combination, and partial overlap between combinations is not counted as agreement.

    1. Normalisation. Each rater's cell is split into labels at line breaks, commas and semicolons. Each label is standardised: non-breaking spaces and tabs become spaces, the ligatures ﬁ and ﬂ are expanded, curly apostrophes and en dashes are replaced by their plain forms, & becomes and, runs of whitespace are collapsed, and the text is trimmed and lower-cased.
    2. Equivalences. Each label is then mapped through the active rules of the equivalence table (below), so that variants such as plurals or typing errors count as the same label. A rule applies to one dimension and stage or to all of them; the most specific rule is used first (dimension and stage, then dimension only, then stage only, then all).
    3. Combination. Repeated labels are removed and the rest are sorted alphabetically and joined. The resulting text is the rater's category for that study and stage.
    4. Observed agreement. For each study i, the proportion of the three rater pairs whose categories match:

      Pi = [ 1(xi1 = xi2) + 1(xi1 = xi3) + 1(xi2 = xi3) ] / 3

      so Pi is 1 when all three agree, 1/3 when exactly two agree and 0 otherwise. The mean over the studies is

      Po = (1/N) Σi Pi

      Studies in which any rater left the stage blank are excluded.
    5. Expected agreement. From the pooled frequencies of the categories over all 3N ratings, where nij is the number of raters assigning study i to category j:

      pj = (1/Nm) Σi nij   ·   Pe = Σj pj2

    6. Kappa. Agreement beyond chance as a fraction of the maximum attainable agreement beyond chance:

      κ = (Po − Pe) / (1 − Pe)

      When every rating falls in a single category (Pe = 1), κ is reported as 1.

    Interpretation

    κStrength of agreement
    < 0.00Poor
    0.00–0.20Slight
    0.21–0.40Fair
    0.41–0.60Moderate
    0.61–0.80Substantial
    0.81–1.00Almost perfect

    Benchmarks proposed by Landis and Koch (1977), who stress that the divisions are arbitrary: they help to compare the relative strength of agreement rather than act as strict thresholds.
