    Definitions of the high-level themes of each dimension

    Tracked digital object dimension (8 themes)

    This dimension focuses on digital artefacts, data entities, and information objects managed, analysed, or protected in the reviewed studies.

    Structured Records
    Structured records stored in predefined fields, including electronic health records (EHR, EMR, and PHR), registration forms, judicial records, financial records, police records, and insurance records.
    Documents
    Textual files that are formatted or semi-structured and intended for human reading, including PDFs, Office files, legal documents, reports, and forms.
    Media Files
    Audiovisual content, including static images, videos, audio files, and diagnostic imaging files (DICOM).
    Data Files
    Structured or semi-structured data files intended for processing by systems, including JSON, XML, CSV, binary files, and data chunks.
    Source Code & CAD
    Software engineering or design artefacts, including source code, CAD files, and computational models.
    Logs & Audit Trails
    System-generated event records, including access logs, audit trails, and transaction histories.
    Metadata
    Data about data, including descriptors, indexes, integrity hashes, access policies, and annotations associated with other objects.
    IoT Data Streams
    Continuous or discrete streams generated by connected devices, sensors, and embedded telemetry.

    Technology dimension (12 themes)

    The technology dimension classifies the technological solutions, frameworks, and methodologies used in the selected studies.

    Cryptography
    A set of cryptographic primitives (symmetric and asymmetric encryption, attribute-based encryption, and digital signatures) and hardware implementations (trusted platform modules and biometrics) used to ensure confidentiality, authenticity, integrity, and revocable access control over digital objects in cloud, Internet of Things, and healthcare environments.
    Blockchain
    Distributed and immutable ledgers, whether public, permissioned, or consortium-based, that provide tamper-evident tracking of access events, consent, and provenance of digital objects, supporting their auditability and traceability. This theme includes non-fungible tokens for encoding property rights and multi-ledger architectures.
    Smart Contracts
    Deterministic programs executed on distributed ledgers that encode and automatically enforce access policies, consent workflows, permission delegation, and regulatory compliance obligations.
    Distributed File System
    Peer-to-peer repositories with content-based addressing, such as the InterPlanetary File System, and abstraction layers, such as virtual file systems, that ensure verifiable retrieval and integrity of digital objects stored off-chain, extending integrity verification, and thus auditability, to off-chain storage environments.
    Steganography & Digital Watermarking
    Content-bound techniques that embed identifiers, provenance metadata, or forensic marks directly into digital objects, including images, documents, and videos, enabling post-delivery forensic attribution even after the object crosses the organisational boundary.
    Machine Learning & AI
    Statistical learning models, including supervised, unsupervised, and neural network approaches, applied to the detection of behavioural anomalies, classification of insider threats, localisation of sensitive data, and evaluation of the robustness of tracking mechanisms.
    Honey Files
    Decoy digital objects that are indistinguishable from legitimate assets and that, when accessed improperly, trigger alerts and generate forensic evidence, enabling attribution of the incident to the responsible party and reducing the value of exfiltrated data.
    Containerisation & Orchestration
    The packaging of services in isolated containers, such as Docker, and their coordination by orchestrators, aiming at scalable, fault-tolerant, and reproducible deployments of microservices.
    Interoperability & Authorisation Standards
    Standardised protocols for delegated authorisation, such as OAuth 2.0, and knowledge representation technologies, including ontologies, RDF, and the Data Privacy Vocabulary, that extend semantic interoperability and the granularity of authorisation in data-sharing ecosystems involving multiple parties.
    Knowledge Representation & Analytics
    Computational methods for the extraction, structuring, and analysis of heterogeneous information, including privacy analysis, behaviour monitoring, optical character recognition for the digitisation of physical documents, and the linkage of geospatial metadata, such as GPS data, to digital objects.
    Digital Forensics Techniques
    Post-incident investigation techniques applied to digital objects and computational systems, with an emphasis on the comparison of operating system records, such as the Windows registry, to identify artefacts related to installation, synchronisation, or tampering.
    Digital Twin
    Dynamic virtual replication of a cyber-physical system that enables continuous monitoring, hypothetical scenario analysis, and real-time threat simulation.

    Purpose dimension (14 themes)

    The purpose dimension categorises studies by their primary aims and intended outcomes.

    Data Traceability
    The ability to follow digital objects throughout their lifecycle, recording their origin, the operations performed on them, and the entity responsible for each operation, so that their provenance and custody can be reconstructed.
    Access Control
    Governance of access to digital objects through policies that define which entities may access which resources, under which conditions, and with which privileges, permission delegation, real-time revocation, and smart-contract-based policy enforcement.
    Data Privacy
    The assurance that personal or sensitive data are collected, processed, and shared in compliance with regulations such as GDPR and LGPD, preserving data subjects' rights to transparency, control, portability, and erasure, with reinforced protection for special data categories through integrated technical and legal safeguards.
    Secure Data Sharing
    The exchange of digital objects between parties while preserving confidentiality, integrity, and authenticity, through secure communication protocols, transport- and content-level encryption, consent management, and guarantees of non-repudiation and atomicity in data transfers.
    Healthcare Data Governance
    Secure, traceable and regulatory-compliant management of EHR, EMR, and PHR records, ensuring that patient data are shared exclusively with authorised entities and remain accessible for care or audit purposes, with support for patient consent, cross-institutional interoperability, and sector-specific compliance.
    Cybersecurity Risk Mitigation
    The reduction of the likelihood and impact of cyberattacks through preventive, detective, and responsive controls throughout the lifecycle of digital objects, encompassing comprehensive defence strategies, threat monitoring, incident response, and resilience of the infrastructure against internal and external threats.
    Data Auditing
    The systematic and independent verification of the states, operations, and usage patterns of digital objects over time, ensuring policy compliance, deviation detection, and integrity assurance through tamper-evident audit trails and record consistency verification.
    Forensic Attribution & Post-Incident Investigation
    Mechanisms enabling ex post investigation of security incidents and forensic attribution of unauthorised disclosures, including digital watermarking, collusion-resistant fingerprinting, audit trail analysis, transaction metadata correlation, and the production of legally admissible evidence.
    Data Confidentiality
    The technical protection of digital objects against unauthorised disclosure through cryptographic primitives, including symmetric and asymmetric encryption, attribute-based encryption (ABE), and proxy re-encryption, binding content access to verifiable credentials or policies independently of perimeter controls.
    Digital Object Lifecycle Management
    The controlled and traceable handling of digital objects across all lifecycle phases, including creation, storage, access, modification, distribution, retention, and deletion, ensuring that each operation is recorded, authorised, and compliant with security policies and regulatory requirements.
    Secure Data Retention
    The secure storage of digital objects in centralised, distributed, or decentralised systems, with guarantees of availability, integrity, and confidentiality, including mechanisms for at-rest integrity verification, repository access control, and retention and deletion policies aligned with regulatory requirements.
    Copyright Protection
    The protection of intellectual property rights over distributed digital objects through mechanisms that bind copies to the rights holder's identity, enabling the detection and proof of unauthorised use through digital watermarking, fingerprinting, immutable provenance registration, and cryptographic resolution of authorship disputes.
    Automated Sensitive Data Detection
    Automatic identification and classification of sensitive information in digital objects or information systems through machine learning, natural language processing, and pattern analysis, enabling proactive identification of content that requires additional protective controls prior to exposure, sharing, or publication.
    Security-Oriented Data Analytics
    Extraction of security-relevant patterns, anomalies, and insights from data related to the use and events of digital objects, supporting proactive monitoring, insider threat detection, risk assessment, and improvement of tracking and accountability mechanisms.

    Domain dimension (11 themes)

    Automotive & Intelligent Transportation
    Addresses secure data sharing, access control, and accountability in vehicular edge computing, connected vehicle ecosystems, and intelligent transportation systems, including vehicle-to-infrastructure and vehicle-to-vehicle communication scenarios.
    Digital Media
    Encompasses the management, protection, and trading of digital media assets, including images, videos, and multimedia files. Studies in this domain focus on copyright protection, secure data transactions, and content-bound accountability mechanisms such as watermarking and steganography applied to audiovisual objects and their distribution chains. Excludes generic security applications without a media-specific context.
    Education
    Explores secure data management, privacy protection, and accountability mechanisms in digital learning environments, including online teaching platforms, learning management systems, and the protection of educational records and sensitive student data.
    Energy & Smart Grid
    Addresses secure and auditable data sharing within smart grid environments, energy informatics systems, and critical energy infrastructure, including accountability mechanisms for energy consumption records, grid telemetry, and access control on energy management platforms.
    Financial Services & Banking
    Addresses data traceability, secure sharing, confidentiality, access control, and accountability in financial services contexts, including banking transactions, open banking frameworks, digital asset management, and audit trails for financial records. Excludes generic technology security solutions not specifically anchored in financial operations or regulatory frameworks.
    Healthcare & Medical Informatics
    Focuses on secure management, sharing, and protection of electronic health records (EHRs), electronic medical records (EMRs), and personal health records (PHRs), as well as sensitive medical imaging and clinical data. Studies address patient-centric consent management, cross-institutional interoperability, and compliance with health sector regulations such as HIPAA and GDPR.
    Industry & Supply Chain
    Covers accountability, traceability, and cybersecurity in industrial manufacturing settings (including Industry 4.0 and additive manufacturing) and complex supply networks, with an emphasis on data auditing, decentralised access control, provenance tracking, and compliance across multi-tier production and logistics chains.
    Legal, Compliance & Digital Forensics
    Addresses management and auditing of legal documents, forensic investigation of digital artefacts, and regulatory compliance in judicial and law enforcement contexts. Includes chain-of-custody mechanisms, evidence integrity, legally admissible audit trails, and accountability frameworks aligned with legal obligations and court procedures.
    Government & Public Sector
    Emphasises secure document management, interoperability, and data protection in electronic government systems, public agencies, smart city platforms, and inter-agency cooperation contexts, including accountability for official government documents and citizen data under public regulatory frameworks.
    Social Platforms
    Focuses on privacy protection, access control, and accountability within online social networks and user-generated content platforms. Distinct from Digital Media in that the emphasis is on user behavioural data, social graph privacy, and protection of personal information shared by individuals in networked social environments, rather than on audiovisual assets and intellectual property.
    Cross-Domain Cybersecurity & Computing
    Studies that address cybersecurity challenges in cloud computing, IoT environments, and general-purpose distributed systems without a primary sectoral application context. Solutions include secure cloud storage, dynamic access control, data auditing, and privacy-preserving data sharing applicable across heterogeneous deployment settings.

    Inter-rater reliability: Fleiss' kappa

    Fleiss' κ for each dimension and coding stage of the thematic analysis, with three raters: Researcher 1 (R1), AI Agent 1 (AI1, GPT-5.2) and AI Agent 2 (AI2, Claude Opus 4.6). The values are recomputed in the browser from the coding sheets with the method described in the article. Select a value of κ to show its calculation below.

    Full agreement counts the studies in which the three raters gave the same complete combination of labels: raw compares the original text character for character, normalised compares the combinations after normalisation and equivalences, and recovered is the difference. Pairwise agreement is the share of studies in which each pair of raters agrees; the mean of the three pairwise rates equals the observed agreement.

    Mean by coding stage

    How it is calculated

    Fleiss' κ measures agreement among m raters who independently assign each of N subjects to nominal categories. Here m = 3 and the subjects are the primary studies. Each dimension allows several labels per study, so each rater's complete combination of labels is treated as one category: agreement means the same complete combination, and partial overlap between combinations is not counted as agreement.

    1. Normalisation. Each rater's cell is split into labels at line breaks, commas and semicolons. Each label is standardised: non-breaking spaces and tabs become spaces, the ligatures ﬁ and ﬂ are expanded, curly apostrophes and en dashes are replaced by their plain forms, & becomes and, runs of whitespace are collapsed, and the text is trimmed and lower-cased.
    2. Equivalences. Each label is then mapped through the active rules of the equivalence table (below), so that variants such as plurals or typing errors count as the same label. A rule applies to one dimension and stage or to all of them; the most specific rule is used first (dimension and stage, then dimension only, then stage only, then all).
    3. Combination. Repeated labels are removed and the rest are sorted alphabetically and joined. The resulting text is the rater's category for that study and stage.
    4. Observed agreement. For each study i, the proportion of the three rater pairs whose categories match:

      Pi = [ 1(xi1 = xi2) + 1(xi1 = xi3) + 1(xi2 = xi3) ] / 3

      so Pi is 1 when all three agree, 1/3 when exactly two agree and 0 otherwise. The mean over the studies is

      Po = (1/N) Σi Pi

      Studies in which any rater left the stage blank are excluded.
    5. Expected agreement. From the pooled frequencies of the categories over all 3N ratings, where nij is the number of raters assigning study i to category j:

      pj = (1/Nm) Σi nij   ·   Pe = Σj pj2

    6. Kappa. Agreement beyond chance as a fraction of the maximum attainable agreement beyond chance:

      κ = (Po − Pe) / (1 − Pe)

      When every rating falls in a single category (Pe = 1), κ is reported as 1.

    Interpretation

    κStrength of agreement
    < 0.00Poor
    0.00–0.20Slight
    0.21–0.40Fair
    0.41–0.60Moderate
    0.61–0.80Substantial
    0.81–1.00Almost perfect

    Benchmarks of Landis and Koch (1977). The authors note that the divisions are arbitrary: they indicate the relative strength of agreement and are not strict thresholds.

    Technology readiness level (TRL)

    Maturity of the solution proposed by each study, rated on the TRL scale of the NASA Technology Readiness Assessment Best Practices Guide. Select a bar or a number to list the studies.

    Assessment procedure

      Criteria by level

      TRL by technology

      A solution can employ more than one technology, so the counts sum to more than the number of solutions. The median is the lower of the two middle values when the number of solutions is even.

      TRL by approach group

      Approach groups of RQ5; a solution can belong to more than one group.

      TRL by publication period

      Rating of each solution

      Evidence on which each rating is based. “DEF + full text” marks the solutions whose full text was consulted.

      Data protection requirements

      Legal texts: GDPR (EUR-Lex) and LGPD, consolidated text (Planalto).

      Correspondence of the requirements in the corpus

      Closest counterparts of each technical requirement among the purposes, technologies, approach groups, architectures and digital objects of the corpus, as a percentage of the primary studies. Grey bands mark requirements without a corresponding theme. Select a bar to list its studies.

      Coverage of the requirements

      The same correspondence as a table (supplementary material of the article): each technical requirement and its closest counterparts in the corpus, with the number and percentage of the primary studies. Select a number to list the studies.

      Heatmaps

      Number of studies coded with both categories, for any two dimensions of the thematic analysis. Select a cell to list its studies.

      Rows and columns are ordered so that the highest count sits in the top-right cell and the lowest counts gather in the bottom-left corner. The column holding the highest count is placed on the right; rows are sorted by their count in that column, highest at the top. Tied rows are separated by their highest count in the remaining columns, then the next highest, and so on. Columns are then sorted by their count in the top row, highest on the right, with ties separated in the same way.
